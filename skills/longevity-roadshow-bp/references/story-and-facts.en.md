# Business Narrative and Facts

[中文版](story-and-facts.md)

## What investors need to judge

| Module | Question to answer | Common useful evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Users and problem | Who faces what problem, in which situation, and how severe is it? | Real interviews, workflows, payment or substitution behavior |
| Product and value | How does the product change that situation? | Demonstration, clear functional scope, reproducible usage results |
| Technology and differentiation | Why does the value hold compared with existing approaches? | Tests under matching conditions, structural explanations, capability limits |
| Market and entry | Who will be served first, and how will they be reached? | Explicit geography/time/population definitions, executable channels |
| Business model | Who pays, how much, and how much cash remains after delivery? | Pricing basis, cost quotes, channel terms |
| Team and progress | Why can this team execute, and what has it completed? | Experience, prototypes, versions, tests, orders, delivery records |
| Execution and financing | Which constraint will this money remove? | Milestones, use of proceeds, acceptance stages, working-capital arrangements |

Do not substitute “disruptive,” “complete closed loop,” or “huge blue ocean” for evidence. If a conclusion cannot be supported, retain the topic and placeholders rather than creating fabricated proof.

## Fact register

Maintain these fields in the working directory; do not automatically add a register slide to the main deck:

`Information item / current wording / fact status / source and date / destination in deck / missing material`

Example fact statuses:

- Evidence available: a specific file or public source.
- Unverified user statement: do not elevate it to a signed deal, revenue, certification, or authorization.
- Planned target: future progress, sales, channels, or iterations.
- Calculation assumption: costs, conversion rates, target-segment filtering ratios, and similar assumptions.
- Interface/appearance example: a design demonstration, not evidence that a product is live or mass-produced.

Keep qualifications needed for a conclusion on the slide. Long citations, design sources, and detailed explanations may go in notes.

## Market and competitors

When external information is required, consult currently verifiable sources, prioritizing industry associations, government data, and manufacturers' official materials. Use research skills and tools available in the current environment.

- Define TAM, SAM, and SOM with matching geography, period, currency, and product scope. An installed stock is not annual revenue.
- Cross-check top-down figures with bottom-up “reachable users/channels × reasonable conversion × revenue per customer.” Mark unsupported ratios as assumptions.
- Do not directly add national population totals, participants in different scenarios, and core paying users.
- Include both similar equipment and users' existing alternatives. Compare user tasks, inputs/outputs, usage conditions, and purchase methods instead of making every row claim universal superiority.
- Separate manufacturer claims from tests under matching conditions. Absence of a feature in public materials does not prove that the feature does not exist.

## Cash and funding

Choose the level of detail from the actual business. Early hardware projects can begin with:

`Cash contribution per unit = actual cash collected − channel settlement − variable delivery cost − acquisition spend`

Variable delivery cost may include the product, packaging, logistics, after-sales support, and return reserves. State whether taxes, R&D, team costs, and fixed overhead are included. Do not label remaining budget headroom as net profit.

Show units sold, order value, units delivered, and actual cash receipts separately. Batch manufacturing also requires consideration of deposits, final payments, payment terms, inventory, and refunds; full-year revenue cannot conceal a short-term cash shortfall.

Fill funding amounts, equity percentages, and valuations only when confirmed for the current project. If the parties use the same ordinary-equity post-money basis for the round, check:

`Post-money valuation = investment in this round ÷ new equity percentage issued in this round`

Retain conditions for convertible debt, staged closings, or other special terms rather than applying the simple formula. Verify specific funding terms or legal/financial judgments separately under the current environment's requirements.

The closing fundraising slide only needs amount, uses, and expected milestones. Uses must sum to the total. Phase budgets and procurement examples must not look like an additional funding round.

## Editable placeholders when information is missing

- Team: `[Name / role / commitment / relevant experience and verifiable achievements]`
- R&D: `[Prototype / algorithm / app stage] [version date] [test results]`
- Users and channels: `[Trial users] [meaningful usage] [coaches/institutions] [orders/deliveries/cash collected]`
- Cost: `[Quote date] [quoted quantity] [unit cost range] [excluded items]`
- Funding: `[Round amount] [equity/other structure] [use of proceeds] [phase targets]`

Brackets identify fields to fill, not accomplished facts. Prioritize established facts and explicitly leave other positions blank where necessary.
